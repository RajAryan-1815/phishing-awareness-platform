🔍 What Was That Email?

That was a simulated phishing email — a fake message designed to mimic a real Google security alert. It contained multiple classic phishing techniques used in real-world attacks every day.

In a real phishing attack, clicking "Verify My Account Now" would take you to a fake Google login page. Any password you typed there would go directly to the attacker — not to Google.

🚩 Red Flags in That Email

1

Misspelled sender domain

The "From" address was security-alerts@accounts-googIe-verify.com. Look closely — it says googIe (capital I) not google (lowercase L). The actual domain is accounts-googIe-verify.com — nothing to do with Google.

2

Extreme urgency and threats

"Verify within 24 hours or your account will be permanently suspended." Real Google security emails give you time, link to Google's own domain, and never threaten immediate permanent suspension via a single email.

3

Generic greeting

"Dear Google User" — Google knows your name. Legitimate emails from Google address you by the name on your account.

4

Suspicious link URL

The "Verify My Account Now" button would lead to a domain that is not google.com. Always hover over links to preview the real destination before clicking.

5

Pressure to act immediately

A countdown timer creates panic and stops you from thinking critically. This is a deliberate psychological manipulation technique used in almost every phishing attack.

✅ What to Do With a Real Email Like This

  1. Do not click any links in the email.
  2. Open a new browser tab and type the official website address manually (e.g., google.com).
  3. Log in through the official site and check if there are any real security alerts.
  4. Report the email as phishing in your email client.
  5. Delete the email.
