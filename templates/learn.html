01

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering attack where cybercriminals pretend to be trustworthy entities — banks, government agencies, tech companies, or even your boss — to trick you into revealing sensitive information or taking a harmful action.

The word "phishing" is a deliberate misspelling of "fishing" because attackers cast a wide net hoping victims will "bite" the hook.

🎣 Real-world example: You receive an email claiming to be from your bank saying your account has been locked. You click a link, land on a convincing fake bank page, and type in your login details — which go straight to the attacker.
02

Common Phishing Techniques

📧
Email Phishing

Mass emails sent to thousands of addresses impersonating a trusted brand.

🎯
Spear Phishing

Targeted attacks using your name, employer or recent activity to seem credible.

📱
Smishing (SMS)

Phishing delivered via text message — "Your parcel could not be delivered, click here."

📞
Vishing (Voice)

Phone calls from fake "bank security teams" or "Microsoft support" agents.

🐋
Whaling

Spear phishing aimed at executives (CEOs, CFOs) to authorise large wire transfers.

🔗
Clone Phishing

A legitimate email is copied, its links replaced with malicious ones, then re-sent.

03

Suspicious Links & URLs

Links are the most common delivery mechanism for phishing attacks. Attackers manipulate URLs so they look legitimate at a glance.

  • Typosquatting — paypa1.com (digit 1 instead of letter l)
  • Subdomain spoofing — paypal.com.evil-site.com (real domain is evil-site.com)
  • URL shorteners — bit.ly/abc123 hides the true destination
  • Homograph attacks — Unicode characters that look identical to Latin letters
💡 Tip: Before clicking any link, hover over it (desktop) or long-press it (mobile) to preview the real URL. If the domain looks wrong — don't click.
04

Urgent / Threatening Language

Attackers exploit fear and urgency to make you act before you think. Genuine organisations give reasonable notice and do not threaten immediate account deletion.

🚨 "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours!"
🔒 "Unusual sign-in detected — verify NOW"
💸 "Your payment failed — update immediately"
⚠️ "Legal action will be taken if you do not respond"
💡 Rule of thumb: The more urgent an email sounds, the more suspicious you should be. Pause, breathe, and verify through the official website.
05

Fake Login Pages

Attackers build pixel-perfect copies of Gmail, banking, or social-media login pages. When you type your credentials, they are sent to the attacker — not the real service.

  • Always check the URL bar — the domain must be exactly right
  • A padlock (HTTPS) does NOT mean the page is legitimate — phishing sites use it too
  • Use a password manager — it won't auto-fill on a fake domain
  • Bookmark important sites and navigate from bookmarks, never from email links
06

Checking Sender Addresses

Display names can say anything. The actual email address is what matters.

❌ Suspicious support@googIe-help.com Capital I instead of lowercase l
❌ Suspicious noreply@amazon.account-verify.net Real domain is account-verify.net, not amazon.com
✅ Legitimate support@amazon.com Official Amazon domain
✅ Legitimate no-reply@accounts.google.com Subdomain of the official google.com domain
07

Dangerous Attachments

Malicious files are disguised as invoices, CVs, or shipping notices. Opening them can install malware silently.

.exe .bat .cmdDirect executables
.docx .xlsxOffice files with macros
.pdfCan contain malicious JS
.zip .rar .isoMay hide executables
  • Never enable macros in Office files from unknown senders
  • Scan attachments with antivirus before opening
  • Be cautious even with known senders — their account may be compromised
08

How to Stay Safe

🔑
Use Strong, Unique Passwords

Use a password manager to generate and store a different password for every site.

📲
Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Even if attackers steal your password, they cannot log in without your second factor.

🔍
Verify Before You Click

Hover over links, inspect sender addresses, and navigate directly to official sites.

🛡️
Keep Software Updated

Security patches close vulnerabilities that phishing payloads try to exploit.

📢
Report Suspicious Messages

Use your email client's "Report Phishing" button — it helps protect everyone.

🧠
Trust Your Instincts

If something feels off, stop. Take a moment to verify before acting.
